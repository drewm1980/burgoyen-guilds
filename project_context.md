## Goal

Redesign the communal garden as a **permaculture food forest** for the Cohousing Bourgoyen project at Nekkerputstraat 176, 9000 Gent, Belgium. The developer's landscape designer (buro Groen) produced a preliminary design that serves as the geometric baseline; the community wants to replace it with a food forest design.

A supporting task is to build a **dimensionally accurate 3D model** of the grounds, suitable for simulating solar exposure (by time of day and season) to inform plant placement decisions.

### Water Management & Drainage Concerns

**Key concern**: Unit 3 (north corner) currently sits below the soil level on the north side of the site. During heavy rainfall, water flows toward the house. The wadis are legally required and are intended to address this, but have not been fully constructed yet. The developer is expected to regrade the entire garden and bring in new topsoil.

**Confirmed by photos** (March 2026): Ground visibly slopes from the northern boundary wall downward toward unit 3's foundation. Estimated drop ~30–60 cm over ~10–15 m. The brick boundary wall ("harde afsluiting") is already constructed and sits noticeably higher than the foundation slab.

**Design response**: A swale running parallel to the north boundary wall, ~2–3 m inward (allowing maintenance access), would intercept runoff at the source. In the food forest this is a productive feature — moisture-loving species on the downhill berm, pioneer shrubs on the mound. This aligns naturally with food forest water management (swales on contour are a core permaculture technique). Regrading is the moment to shape the land correctly — berms and swales can be integrated into the food forest layout.

## Site Boundary & Surroundings

The cohousing project occupies the interior of a **city block**. The PDF drawings include the surrounding plots of neighboring properties. These are separated from the cohousing garden by **brick walls or tall fences** — they are neighboring backyards, not part of the design scope.

For the 3D solar model, the surrounding walls/fences are shadow-casting elements that should be included in the geometry, even though the vegetation behind them is outside scope.

## Neighboring Context (from T_N_005_Profiel Perceelsgrenzen, page 24)

The site is surrounded on all sides by **2–3 story rijhuizen** (terraced houses), approximately 6–9m tall. The annotated values on page 24 appear to be ground level differences and boundary wall/fence heights at the property line, not building ridge heights.

**Photo reference**: `docs/view of north corner of the building site...IMG_20260329_163016291_HDR.jpg` (taken March 2026) shows the garden as a completely bare, flat construction site. Confirms: neighbors are 2–3 story rijhuizen on all sides, garden is lower than surrounding properties, church tower visible to NE. Full blank canvas — regrading and topsoil needed.

**Wind implication**: The surrounding rijhuizen form a significant windbreak on all sides. The NW/NE garden zones are relatively sheltered compared to an open site. Wind exposure is likely moderate.

**Shadow implication**: Neighboring rijhuizen are visibly taller than the new cohousing houses and will cast shadows into the site in winter. Must be included in the solar model.

**Ground level offset**: The cohousing site sits approximately **0.5–1.0 m higher** than the surrounding neighboring properties (values on page 22 profile: −0.54 to −0.99 m). The annotated negative values on the boundary profiles are ground level differences in metres, not building heights. Effective shadow height of neighbors = their building height minus ~0.7 m offset.

## 3D Model & Solar Simulation Plan

### Objective
Build a Python-based pipeline to:
1. Georeference the AutoCAD plan and extract building footprints.
2. Extrude buildings to 3D using known TAW heights.
3. Calculate solar position for Gent across the year using `pvlib`.
4. Cast shadow rays onto a garden-surface grid to produce seasonal irradiance maps.
5. Add parametric food forest tree models to simulate canopy shading at maturity.

### Key technical facts for the model
- **Coordinate reference**: TAW (Belgian altitude datum). Ground ≈ +9.0 TAW.
- **Drawing scale**: 1:200 (confirmed from both PDFs); phase DO (Definitief Ontwerp) — authoritative final design
- **Dimension units**: All labeled dimensions on permit drawings are in **cm** unless otherwise noted
- **North orientation**: **North = up (+y) on the permit plan (page 2)**. East = right (+x). The drawing is north-aligned.
- **Sun position library**: `pvlib` with `Location(latitude=51.0626, longitude=3.6904, tz='Europe/Brussels')`.
- **Shadow geometry**: `trimesh` for ray-mesh intersection; `shapely` for 2D footprint tracing.
- **Visualization**: `pyvista` (3D interactive) + `matplotlib` (2D heatmaps).

### PDF coordinate system (permit plan, page 2)
- Page size: **2384 × 1684 PDF points** (A1 landscape)
- Scale conversion: **1 PDF point = 0.07056 m** (= 1/72 inch × 200)
- Inverse: **14.17 PDF points = 1 metre**
- Origin: bottom-left of page. x increases east, y increases north.
- Title block (schaal, datum, etc.) sits at x ≈ 2288–2384, y ≈ 43–484 (bottom-right corner).
- Surrounding cadastral parcel areas visible as labels (e.g. 6039 m², 1195 m²) — these are neighbouring plots, not site dimensions.

### Phases
1. **Phase 1** — Georeference & calibrate: establish PDF-point → meter transform using scale bar or known dimension.
2. **Phase 2** — Trace footprints: click-trace building outlines as `shapely` polygons in a Jupyter notebook.
3. **Phase 3** — Extrude to 3D: buildings become `trimesh` meshes using TAW heights above grade.
4. **Phase 4** — Solar simulation: `pvlib` sun angles + ray casting per garden grid cell.
5. **Phase 5** — Food forest layer: add parametric trees (position, mature height, canopy radius) and re-run.
