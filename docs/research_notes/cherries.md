# Cherry pair: research notes

Goal: two sweet cherry trees in open ground; early ripening; low loss to mold, birds and disease; pale fruit if possible.
Status: research only, not yet reflected in the deck. Unverified points are flagged.

## 1. Pollination compatibility (S-alleles)

Published S-genotypes (from search summaries of German Fruit Gene Bank and related papers; the primary PDFs were not readable, so treat as secondary):

| Variety | S-alleles | Group | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Burlat | S3S9 | XVI | not self-fertile |
| Early Rivers | S1S2 | I | not self-fertile |
| Bigarreau Napoleon | S3S4 | III | not self-fertile |
| Stella | S3S4 (self-fertile mutation) | III | self-fertile |
| Karina | reported S3S4, group III | III | **unverified**; fruitbomen.net lists it as self-fertile |
| Sunburst | not found | - | fruitbomen.net: "fairly self-fertile" |
| Donissens Gelbe / Gold | not found | - | not self-fertile |

Key points:
- **Burlat (S3S9) + Early Rivers (S1S2) share no S-allele, so they are fully cross-compatible.**
- Burlat (S3S9) + Napoleon (S3S4) share S3, which normally means only half the pollen is compatible, although nursery tables list Napoleon as a "best" partner for Burlat.
- Napoleon and Stella are both S3S4 (same group) and should not pollinate each other, although fruitbomen.net lists Stella as a "best" partner for Napoleon. That is a discrepancy in the nursery table, not confirmed by the genotype data.
- Bloom timing: no per-variety bloom dates were found. fruitbomen.net says sweet cherries bloom within days to weeks of each other and its pollination tables give no bloom groups. Napoleon blooms late March (French Wikipedia). Overlap is unconfirmed for every candidate pair.
- Nursery tables: https://fruitbomen.net/informatie/bestuiving/5/kersenbomen/bigarreau-burlat (also the `bigarreau-napoleon` and `donissens-gold` pages under the same path).

## 2. What is 'Bigarreau blanc et rose'?

Not confirmed. French nursery pages describe Napoleon (= Royal Ann, Lauermann in some catalogues) as yellow with a pink/red blush and white firm flesh ("jaune rose"). That fits a "white and pink" name, but no source found lists "blanc et rose" as a synonym (French Wikipedia lists none). Plausible but unproven that the existing tree is Napoleon. Cheapest test: have a Belgian pomologist identify it from fruit and leaves; Napoleon ripens about late June and blooms late March.
Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerise_bigarreau_Napol%C3%A9on, https://www.silencecapousse-chezvous.fr/cerisier-bigarreau-napoleon-lauermann, https://www.promessedefleurs.com/fruitiers/fruitiers-par-variete/cerisier/cerisier-bigarreau-napoleon.html

## 3. Ripening

- Burlat: very early (cherry week 1-2, late May to mid June in Belgium, per fruitbomen.net).
- Early Rivers: early (cherry week 2-3), small soft dark fruit.
- Sunburst, Stella: early-mid (not date-checked). Stella cracks in heavy rain per a US grower guide.
- Napoleon: late June (French Wikipedia); "good-tasting early cherry" per fruitbomen.net. Yellow with red blush, so birds likely target it once the blush shows (see section 5).
- Donissens Gelbe / Gold: mid-July (cherry week 5-6, German nurseries); fruitbomen.net says August. **Not early.** Not crack-resistant (Baumschule Eggert), which sits poorly with the wet-weather goal.
- **No early, clearly yellow/white cherry with a verified partner was found.** The pale options are mid-season (Donissens) or bicolor (Napoleon).

## 4. Cracking, brown rot, canker

- 'Early Burlat' is described as resistant to cracking and bacterial canker by a nursery (sales copy). A scientific study (Ilicic et al. 2018, J. Phytopathology) found "Bigarreau Burlat" highly susceptible to bacterial canker. Whether these are the same clone is unclear, so treat Burlat's canker resistance as **contested**.
- Sunburst showed no bacterial spot symptoms in that field study.
- No comparative brown rot (Monilia) data for these varieties was found. General point: fruit wounds from cracking, hail and spotted-wing drosophila are entry points.
- Sources: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jph.12763, https://fruitsandshoots.com/products/sweet-cherry-early-burlat

## 5. Birds and spotted-wing drosophila (SWD)

- Birds: a grower/extension source says clear yellow cherries "may escape much bird damage because birds cannot pick them out easily unless red spots appear; yellow-red types are attacked as soon as the red blush shows". Practical reading: fully yellow (Donissens) plausible; bicolor (Napoleon) not reliably protected. Anecdotal, no controlled trial found. https://goodfruit.com/saving-cherries-from-the-birds/
- SWD: infestation was higher in late-ripening than early-ripening cultivars in mainland Greece, but in coastal Greece both were highly susceptible; overwintered females infest the earliest fruit. So early ripening helps in some settings only. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7564280/ and https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6315960/
- A lab study found the most SWD eggs on Burlat (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11679544/). Higher sugar and softer skin both raise SWD risk (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13075821/).

## 6. Recommendation (tentative)

- **Option A (best supported): Burlat + Early Rivers.** Fully cross-compatible genotypes, both early, both sold in Belgium. Cost: both dark red; Burlat canker and SWD reports mixed.
- **Option B (least risk of no fruit): one self-fertile early tree (Sunburst or Stella)**, ideally with a second variety or the existing tree as extra pollen. Stella cracks in rain.
- **Option C (pale, accepts later ripening): Donissens Gold + a compatible partner.** fruitbomen.net lists Sylvia, Hedelfinger, Regina, Stella, Napoleon and others as best partners. Ripens mid-July to August and is not crack-resistant, which conflicts with "avoid late".

Open: no fully satisfying early + yellow + verified-partner option found. Confirm bloom overlap with a nursery before buying.
