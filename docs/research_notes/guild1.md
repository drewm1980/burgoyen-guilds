## 3. Cercis
- **Age to first bloom**: no reliable figure for nursery container or grafted 'Bodnant'. Found only: flowers form on year-old-or-older wood and the trunk; 'Bodnant' is slow, about 3 m x 1.5 m at 10 years; seed-grown about 15 years to first bloom (single source, from earlier). https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/81956/cercis-siliquastrum-bodnant/details , https://www.vdberk.com/trees/cercis-siliquastrum-bodnant/ [S]. A "time to maturity 20-50 years" line appears; no first-flower age.
  - Implication: the deck's 6-8 m looks too big for the first decade; recheck mature height (5-10 m per Bomen & Enzo).
- **Nitrogen**: Cercis is in subfamily Cercidoideae, a non-nodulating legume group; redbud is listed as non-nodulated [S]. https://nph.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/nph.14474 , https://www.indefenseofplants.com/blog/2017/4/12/meet-the-redbuds . Fairly confident it does not fix nitrogen.

## 5. Other small juglone-tolerant plants (shade / wet where noted); all from black-walnut tolerance lists, none tested with heartnut [S]
- Elderberry (*Sambucus*; red elder tolerates shade, likes moist): edible (cooked berries, flowers)
- Hazel (*Corylus*): edible nuts; plant 2+ (from species.toml)
- Dogwood (*Cornus sericea* red-osier; alternate-leaf dogwood in shade): ornamental; moist soil OK
- Buttonbush (*Cephalanthus occidentalis*): needs moist to wet soil; pollinator plant; ornamental (a wadi candidate; Ghent hardiness unchecked)
- Willow / alder / poplar: listed tolerant, wet-soil trees; too large for the spot (alder also fixes nitrogen; check size and invasiveness)
- Viburnum (e.g. guelder rose *V. opulus*): ornamental/wildlife, moist soil OK
- Sources: https://chiefrivernursery.com/shop-by-use/juglone-tolerant.html , https://vinlandvalleynursery.com/2021/12/walnut-tolerant-plants/ , https://www.wasconursery.com/plants-resistant-to-juglone/
- Caveat: lists are mostly North American natives, not tested for European cultivars.

## 6. Knotweed suppression evidence; walnut pollen
- **No source found showing that heartnut or walnut juglone or shade suppresses Japanese knotweed.** Juglone-on-knotweed effect: unverified.
- **Competitive-planting studies exist, for other plants**:
  - *Salix viminalis* (willow) mesocosm study: knotweed still survived under dense willow canopy, but its performance and spread were significantly reduced. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0301479718311228
  - Native-species mixtures vs. reinfestation: two years of prior knotweed suppression (glyphosate/mowing) were needed before native mixes could compete. https://er.uwpress.org/content/30/3/192.abstract
  - Belgian control tests: "Can land managers control Japanese knotweed? Lessons from control tests in Belgium" https://www.academia.edu/32390053/Can_Land_Managers_Control_Japanese_Knotweed_Lessons_from_Control_Tests_in_Belgium (not opened)
  - Knotweed has allelopathic effects on willow/cottonwood cuttings: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0301479713006774
  - Implication: the deck's "dense shade and juglone slow knotweed" rests on general shade and competition, not on evidence for walnut. Consider softer wording ("may slow", "helps once knotweed is knocked back") or citing the willow result.
- **J. regia pollen on J. ailantifolia**: no direct evidence found. General: *J. regia* crosses with many species and can act as a "bridge" across sections (Pollegioni et al. 2013, https://www.fs.usda.gov/nrs/pubs/jrnl/2013/nrs_2013_pollegioni_001.pdf), but the regia x ailantifolia pollination itself is not documented in what I read. Still unconfirmed.
